\begin{abstract}
Natural language model outputs are attractive verification objects because they
are portable, public, and easy to copy.  They are also a weak substrate for
deterministic provenance claims: the logs, proofs, model state, and decoding
events that make evidence strong often do not travel with the text.  We study
this verification substrate gap using first-divergence token-event diagnostics.
Under provider-side trace access, protected codewords are recovered in
94/96 Qwen blocks and 96/96 Llama blocks, with raw, task-only, wrong-key, and
wrong-payload controls at 0/96.  However, the corresponding public final text
recovers zero codeword blocks.  Shallow public final-text predicates show some
distributional separability, but they are searchable, editable, learnable, and
directly optimizable by source-mismatch examples; guided rewrite/graft attacks
reach 100/100 accepted top-ranked source-mismatch rows for the tested Qwen raw,
Qwen task-only, and Llama raw sources.  These results support a substrate-gap
claim: provider-side traces can expose controllable evidence, while current
public final-text predicates do not provide public text-only verification
success, natural evidence success, or an ownership proof.
\end{abstract}
